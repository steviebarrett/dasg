Restored text:

< so >

1.    <Atá fán> tulaigh so tuaidh
            mac mhic Cumhaill as cruaidh col<g>,
        <mac déidgheal> nighin[e] an Deirg
            nach tug rā fheirg brēithir borb.    (4)

2.    <Atá> fán tulaigh so deas
         mac mhic Dhuibhne, c<h>neas mar bhláth;
        cha d’éar sé neach fā ní,
            an greis nochar mhín a l<ámh>.    (8)

3.    <A>tá fā[n] tulaigh [so] thoir
           Osgar bu mhath goil is gníomh;
       Clann Morn[a], gé math na fir,
           nochar chuir sé sin <am> bríogh.    (12)

 4.    Atá fā[n] tulaigh so thiar
           gille bu mhian leis na <mnáibh>;
       mac Rōnáin dār <bhuidheach> cliar
           fán tulaigh so thiar <atá>.     (16)

 5.    Atá fán tulaigh so fúm
           aoinfhear bu <mhór> grúig is <gráin>;
       Conán do gheobh[adh] gach muirn
           fā[n] tulaigh [so] fúm atá.    (20)

 

Translation:
<     this     >

1.      Beneath this mound to the north lies Cumhall’s grandson, whose sword was hard; the white-toothed son of the daughter of Dearg, who uttered no rude word in wrath.

2.      Beneath this mound to the south lies Duibhne’s grandson, whose skin was like blossom; he did not refuse anything to anyone, and his hand was not gentle in conflict.

3.      Beneath this mound to the east lies Osgar, who was good in prowess and deed; he held the family of Morna of no account, although it consisted of fine men.

4.      Beneath this mound to the west lies a lad whom women desired; Rónán’s son, to whom poets were <thankful>, lies under this western mound.

5.      Beneath this mound below me lies a man of great displeasure and loathsomeness; Conán, who received every kind of favour, lies under this mound below me.
